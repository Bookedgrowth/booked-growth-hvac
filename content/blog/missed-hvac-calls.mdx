Most HVAC owners think they answer almost every call. The phone is in their pocket. The office is open. The techs pick up when they can.

The data says otherwise. According to [WhatConverts](https://www.whatconverts.com/?p=35621), **more than 1 in 4 calls to HVAC businesses goes unanswered.**

That's not a small leak. If you get 100 calls a month, that could be 25 people who wanted to give you money and never reached you. And most of them won't call back. They'll call the next company on Google.

This guide covers why calls get missed, what each missed call really costs and five fixes you can start this week.

## Why HVAC calls get missed

Missed calls are rarely about lazy staff. They happen because of how HVAC work runs.

**Your techs are on a job.** A tech with their hands in an air handler can't answer a call. If their cell is your main number, that call goes to voicemail.

**The office is slammed.** The first hot week of the year, every line rings at once. One person can only take one call at a time.

**It's after hours.** AC units love to quit at night and on weekends. If nobody picks up at 9pm, the homeowner keeps scrolling.

**Calls ring to the wrong place.** Your website shows an old number. Your Google profile shows another. Your truck wrap shows a third. Some of those may not ring anywhere useful.

**Nobody knows it's happening.** Most shops don't track missed calls. If you can't see the problem, you can't fix it.

## What a missed call really costs

A missed call isn't free. You paid to make that phone ring.

Maybe you paid for a Google ad. Maybe you paid for a Local Services Ads lead, a wrapped truck, a yard sign or years of good work that earned a referral. Every one of those costs money and time.

When the call goes unanswered, all of that spend is gone. Worse, it often goes straight to a competitor, who now has a new customer for life.

Think about the full value of one customer, not just one repair. A homeowner who calls for an AC repair may later buy a maintenance plan, refer a neighbor or need a new system. One missed call can be worth far more than the job in front of you.

## Fix 1: Turn on missed-call text back

This is the fastest win. When a call goes unanswered, an automatic text goes out within seconds:

> "Hi, this is Your Company Heating & Air. Sorry we missed you! Want us to call you right back, or can we help by text?"

Many people would rather text than leave a voicemail. A quick reply keeps the homeowner talking to you instead of dialing the next company. Many phone systems and CRMs can do this today. Just make sure your texts follow the rules for business messaging, including an easy way to opt out.

## Fix 2: Use one number everywhere

Pick one main number and use it on your website, Google Business Profile, ads, trucks and invoices. If you use call tracking numbers for ads, make sure they all forward to the same place.

This makes calls easier to route and easier to measure. It also helps your Google profile, because your business info matches across the web.

## Fix 3: Plan for the rush

You know the heat waves are coming. Plan for them before they hit.

- Set up call routing so a busy line rolls to a second person.
- Consider an answering service for overflow and after-hours calls.
- Give your CSRs a short script for booking fast, so calls don't drag on.

Even small changes help. If one person can book a call in two minutes instead of five, you can handle a lot more calls in a busy hour.

## Fix 4: Let people book without calling

Not every homeowner wants to talk on the phone. Some would rather tap a time slot on your website or send a text.

Add online booking or a text-to-book button to your site. Put it next to your phone number on every page. That way, even if every line is busy, the homeowner still has a way to reach you right now.

A good [HVAC website](/services/website-design) makes this simple: tap-to-call in the header, a sticky call button on phones and a booking option for people who don't want to call.

## Fix 5: Track every call

You can't fix what you can't see. Call tracking shows you:

- How many calls came in
- How many were answered and how many were missed
- Which ad, page or listing made the phone ring
- Which calls turned into booked jobs

This matters most when you run ads. If you're paying for clicks or leads, you need to know which ones turn into real work. That's why we track [cost per booked job](/services/paid-ads), not just clicks.

## A simple weekly check

Once a week, spend 10 minutes on these questions:

1. How many calls did we miss?
2. When did we miss them? (Time of day, day of week)
3. Did every missed call get a text back?
4. How many missed calls did we win back?

Patterns show up fast. Maybe you miss most calls between noon and 2pm. Maybe Saturdays are the problem. Once you see the pattern, the fix is usually simple.

## The bottom line

More marketing won't help if your phone keeps ringing out. Before you spend more on ads, make sure the calls you already get are answered, or at least get a fast text back.

Fix the leaks first. Then turn up the spend. That's how you turn more calls into a fuller board.
