**Short answer: most HVAC companies should start with Local Services Ads, then add Google Search ads once calls are flowing.** LSAs usually cost less per lead and need less setup. Search ads give you more control and more reach once your foundation is ready.

But "most" isn't "all." This guide explains how each ad type works, what recent data says about cost, and how to decide what's right for your company.

## What are Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads (LSAs) are the boxes at the very top of Google for searches like "AC repair near me." They show your company name, star rating, years in business and a blue "Google Verified" check mark.

A few things make LSAs different:

- **You pay per lead, not per click.** You're charged when a homeowner calls or messages you through the ad, not when they just look.
- **Google checks you first.** You need to pass a background check and show your license and insurance before your ads can run.
- **Reviews matter a lot.** Your Google reviews and how fast you respond to leads help decide how often you show up.
- **You can dispute bad leads.** If a lead is spam or for a service you don't offer, you can ask Google for a credit.

LSAs don't send people to your website. The homeowner calls or messages you right from the ad.

## What are Google Search ads?

Google Search ads are the text ads that show under the LSAs and above the regular results. They're marked "Sponsored."

With Search ads:

- **You pay per click.** You're charged when someone clicks your ad, whether they call or not.
- **You pick the searches.** You choose keywords like "AC repair," "furnace install" or "water heater replacement," and you can block searches you don't want.
- **You write the ad and choose the page.** People click through to your website or a landing page, so that page has to do the selling.
- **You get more control.** You can set budgets by service, by area and by time of day.

## How the costs compare

Lead costs change by city, season and competition, so treat any number as a rough guide. That said, recent HVAC data is helpful.

According to SearchLight data shared by [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/hvac-marketing-complete-guide-2026):

- **Google Local Services Ads averaged about $51 per HVAC lead in February 2026, and 44% of those leads booked a job.**
- **Non-branded Google Search ads averaged about $149 per HVAC lead in January 2026.**

"Non-branded" means searches that don't include your company name, like "AC repair near me." Those are the searches you need to win new customers.

So on average, LSA leads cost about a third as much as non-branded Search leads in that data. That's the main reason we usually start with LSAs.

## When to start with Local Services Ads

LSAs are usually the right first step if:

- You have a decent number of recent Google reviews.
- You can answer calls fast, or at least text back right away.
- Your website isn't ready for paid traffic yet.
- You want to test ads without a big setup.

Because LSAs lean on your reviews, a strong [Google Business Profile](/services/google-business-profile) helps a lot. If you only have a handful of reviews, start a review system now. It will help your LSAs, your Maps ranking and your trust with homeowners.

## When to start with Google Search ads

Search ads may make sense first if:

- LSAs aren't available for your service or area.
- You want to push one service hard, like system replacements.
- You already have a fast website with tap-to-call and good service pages.
- You've maxed out the leads LSAs can give you.

Search ads also let you target high-value jobs on purpose. LSA leads can be a mix of small repairs and big jobs. With Search ads, you can put more budget behind "AC replacement" and less behind "AC filter."

## Why the best answer is usually both

LSAs and Search ads don't compete with each other. They show in different spots on the same page. Running both means you take up more of the screen, and the homeowner sees your name twice before they scroll.

A common plan looks like this:

1. **Get the foundation ready.** Strong Google profile, a fast site and call tracking.
2. **Start LSAs.** Learn which services and areas bring the best leads.
3. **Add Search ads.** Target the jobs and areas where you want more volume.
4. **Add Meta ads later.** Build demand with tune-up and maintenance offers.

## How to judge if your ads are working

Don't judge ads by clicks or even by leads alone. Judge them by **booked jobs** and **cost per booked job.**

A $51 lead that books a job 44% of the time is a very different deal than a $51 lead that never books. That's why every ad should have call tracking, and why you should check which calls turned into work on your board.
